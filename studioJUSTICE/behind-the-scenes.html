Where It Begins

The Creative Process

Every project at Studio Justice starts with a conversation — not about cameras or shot lists, but about feeling. I want to know what a client wants people to feel when they watch the final film. That emotional core becomes the compass for every decision that follows, from location choices to color grades to the rhythm of the edit.

Before a single frame is captured, I spend significant time in pre-production: building mood boards, scouting locations, studying the light at different times of day, and writing out detailed shot lists that balance spontaneity with intentionality. I believe you can only improvise brilliantly when you know exactly what you're after.

This preparation isn't just about efficiency on set — it's about creating space for magic. When the technical scaffolding is locked in, I'm free to be present, to notice the unexpected and lean into it. The best moments in any film I've ever shot weren't planned. They were earned by being prepared enough to recognize them when they happened.

Vision & Philosophy

Light, Movement, and Feeling

I've always been drawn to films that are more felt than watched. Cinema at its best bypasses the analytical mind and speaks directly to something deeper — a memory, a longing, a sense of recognition. That's the standard I hold my own work to. Not "did it look good?" but "did it move someone?"

My visual language is shaped by a love of Italian cinema — the patience of the long take, the confidence of negative space, the way great directors let silence do the heavy lifting. I apply those principles whether I'm shooting a three-minute music video or a feature-length documentary. Scale changes, but intention doesn't.

Color plays an enormous role in how I see storytelling. I spend as much time in the color suite as I do on set because a grade isn't just a stylistic finish — it's an emotional layer. Warm amber shadows can make a scene feel like memory; desaturated blues can make vulnerability feel clinical. Every palette I choose is a choice about how the viewer will inhabit the story.

The work I'm proudest of doesn't announce itself. It draws you in quietly, holds you, and leaves you with something you can't quite name but know you needed. That's the target. Every project is a chance to get a little closer to it.

How We Work

From Idea to Final Cut

01
Discovery Call
We talk through your vision, timeline, and goals. I ask a lot of questions. The more I understand what you're after, the better the film will be.
02
Pre-Production
Location scouting, mood boarding, shot list creation, call sheet preparation, and coordinating any talent, permits, or supporting crew needed.
03
Production Day(s)
We shoot. I'm methodical but fluid — working the shot list while staying alert to the unexpected moments that often become the best ones.
04
Edit & Delivery
Offline edit, color grade, audio mix, and sound design. You receive a review cut, we refine, and then the final deliverable is yours — mastered for every platform.
