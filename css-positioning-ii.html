Wandering Light — A Poem

I walked along the morning shore,
where waves forget what came before,
and sea glass catches early sun.

The tide pulled back without a word,
no footprint held, no echo heard,
just foam retreating, always done.

A pelican dove sharp and clean,
the only witness to the scene,
and left no shadow where he'd been.

The desert bloomed in April rain,
each cactus wren its own refrain,
the red rocks warm with morning light.

A hawk hung still against the blue,
that effortless and patient view,
surveying canyons left and right.

The sagebrush bent, the dust rose thin,
the world outside like world within,
both vast, both patient, both alight.

In autumn fields the corn stood tall,
then leaned, then bowed, then chose to fall,
gold upon the damp black ground.

The farmer walked those quiet rows,
hands deep in pockets, thinking slow,
grateful for the harvest found.

A crow called out from somewhere far,
beneath the early evening star,
the only voice, the only sound.

Snow came late and stayed too long,
pressed the pine boughs, hushed the song
of every bird that used to call.

The children watched from frosted glass,
and traced their breath as minutes passed,
while white replaced the world of fall.

Then came the thaw and mud and green,
the tulip pushing through unseen,
the yard alive with winter's end.

And every year the same surprise,
the same light caught in children's eyes,
as seasons turn and warmth descends.
